Privacy

Almost nothing to disclose.
That is the point.

Updated 23 August 2026. Written in plain language on purpose.

This websiteGoogle Analytics runs on these pages to count visits in aggregate — which pages are read, from which country, on what kind of device. It sets its own cookies to do that, and Google sees the requests your browser makes to it. The pages themselves are static: nothing you type reaches us, and there are no advertising trackers. Fonts load from Google Fonts, and film players load from YouTube's privacy-enhanced domain when you press play; those services see the requests your browser makes to them.
The trial formIf you request a trial we receive what you type: name, e-mail, company, role and your message. It is used to review and answer your request and for nothing else — never sold, never added to a marketing list. Write to us and it is deleted.
E-mailIf you e-mail us, we have your e-mail. We use it to reply.
The application itselfEngineering Workspace runs on the operating company's own infrastructure. Project documents, the index, the database, mail tokens and AI keys live there, under that company's control — not on our servers. Per-user secrets are encrypted; roles and an audit trail govern access. We could not read your project record if we wanted to: we do not have it.
AI usageAI tasks run on the AI account the workspace or the user connects. Prompts for those tasks go to that AI provider under that provider's terms; local features — search, registers, workflows, studios, calculators — send nothing anywhere.
The licence check-inA licensed or trial install contacts our licence service about once a day to renew its licence: it sends the app version, a machine code (a hash — not your hardware serials), the computer's name and the count of documents and pages indexed. Never a file name, never document content, never a search query. If the service is unreachable the app simply keeps working on its current licence; if you stop the check-ins the licence stops renewing and the workspace winds down gently to read-only — nothing is ever deleted remotely.
