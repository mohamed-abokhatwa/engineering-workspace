The check that runs before anything leaves

Your own documents already know
what is wrong with that letter.

An RFI, a letter, a submittal cover, a claim notice. Paste it in and the workspace retrieves the clauses it relies on — the whole section, not the sentence somebody remembered — and answers six questions before the Engineer gets the chance to.

01

Do the clauses say what you claim?

Every specification section, drawing and code the draft leans on is retrieved and read. A quotation that does not survive its own source is found here, not in the response.

02

Is there a clause that contradicts you?

The one nobody thinks to look for: a requirement elsewhere in the same section that forbids exactly what you are proposing. A draft claiming compliance while breaching an unmentioned clause is worse than one that asks openly.

03

Is that the edition your contract binds?

A standing register of every code the contract binds, at which edition, against which edition is actually on your shelf. A section number taken from a later edition is a rejection that looks like a technicality and costs a full cycle.

04

Are you deviating without saying so?

Undeclared deviations are what turn a technical discussion into a credibility problem. Declared ones get approved. The check names each one and drafts the sentence that declares it.

05

Have you made a claim one example destroys?

"No longer available anywhere" invites a reviewer to spend ten minutes disproving you, and then to read the rest of your submission with suspicion. It is replaced with what was surveyed, when, and by whom.

06

Is there a required response date?

Without one, a six-week silence leaves no position worth arguing. The check refuses to call a draft ready until it says when an answer is needed and what that answer protects.

Asked before the trial

Plain answers first.

What is EngSpace, in one sentence?EngSpace, the Engineering Workspace, is a project intelligence platform for engineering and construction offices: it reads your contract, specifications, drawings and correspondence, checks what you are about to issue against them, and keeps registers, sign-off workflows, claims evidence, drawing studios and a client portal around that one record, on a server you own.
Where does my project data live?On your own infrastructure. Documents, the search index and the database stay on the machine your company operates; the engine reads, OCRs and answers locally. Remote access is off until you switch it on; while it is on it runs through an encrypted tunnel and sign-in is always required. Nothing is stored in a vendor cloud.
What does it cost?Two ways in. One engineer on one project pays $39 a month (or $390 a year) and gets every feature. A company pays one annual price for the workspace with unlimited engineers and projects. There is no per-seat bill, no per-module unlock, and no charge for the calculators or the studios. AI tasks run on your own AI key, so there is no per-question fee to us. The full picture is on the pricing page, and a free 15-day trial comes first.
Does it read Arabic documents?Yes. Scanned Arabic letters and stamped drawings are OCRed and become searchable alongside English, and the interface itself ships with a full Arabic right-to-left option.
How does the AI stay honest?Every answer must cite its source, document and page. When the evidence is not there, the assistant writes TBC instead of inventing, and a checker pass tries to prove drafts wrong before you see them. Search, registers, workflows and calculators are deterministic local features that need no AI account at all.
How should I test it?Request the trial — it installs on your own machine, so you can point it at the real project without anything leaving your computer — and try to break it: ask questions where two clauses conflict, where the wrong code edition is cited, or where a previous Engineer response changed the interpretation. Catching those is the whole point of the outbound check.
What does the trial install need from my machine?A 64-bit machine with 8 GB of memory (16 GB is comfortable) and room on an SSD for your documents plus about 15% for the index. The installer carries everything else — runtime, search models, Arabic-English OCR — and the first run benchmarks your computer and tells you what it can comfortably carry. The details are on the requirements page.
